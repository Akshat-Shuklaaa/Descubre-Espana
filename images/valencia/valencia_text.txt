PARA :
Valencia’s culture is best tasted and heard. Valencian and Spanish live side by side on its streets, while old crafts like ceramics and traditional dress keep its heritage alive. At the table, paella and orxata were born here, joined by fideuà, black rice and sweet fartons, all meant to be shared slowly and with company.

FESTIVALS AND CELEBRATIONS
Las Fallas
"Built all year, burned in a single night."
Cardboard and wood monuments, thunderous mascletàs, flower offerings and brass bands, all ending in the blaze of La Cremà. A UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage festival.
La Tomatina
"One town, one morning, and a sea of red."
Last Wednesday of August, in Buñol: a giant street tomato battle.

WHAT I LEARNED
Working on this guide taught me that Valencia is much more than a coastal city. I learnt that paella and orxata were born here, that Valencian and Spanish share the streets, and that the Falles are built all year to burn in a single night. I felt amazed by how a whole city can put so much heart into its festivals, food and traditions. Learning about the mascletà made me curious to feel that thunder myself, and La Tomatina made me smile. Most of all, I felt that Valencia is a place where people take their time, share their table and celebrate together.
